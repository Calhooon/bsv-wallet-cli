## Revision of 2026-11-03

**The owner's ruling, relayed verbatim by the stack's keeper:** "okay I like your reccomendations then....." on the keeper's recommendations W1 (a) and W4 (a). **W1 (a):** the grant's scope is the reference's four permission kinds (protocol, basket, certificate, spending), granted separately per origin as `WalletPermissionsManager` grants them at the pin, least privilege, every power one row in the store. **W4 (a):** a page that disconnects while its prompt is open is denied and no row is kept, the reference's behaviour.

The document of PR 439 chose the opposite on both: one `wallet` row per origin admitting all 28 doors (W1), and the pending row kept after a disconnect (W4). L1 to L4 built that shape and moved no page's admission (the gate still reads `ALLOWED_ORIGINS`). What moved here:

- **(1) the store:** the scopes `wallet` and `spending` give way to the four kinds, each row one power keyed by (originator, kind, key) in a canonical text form; the `source` column and the env rows are withdrawn; the migration of L1's tables; the door-to-kind map over the 28 doors and the host routes, with the open doors as the reference has them (a new witness row, W15, for the owner).
- **(2) the pending answer:** a disconnect deletes the originator's open rows and denies every call held on them; a restart sweeps open rows; the cap shrinks and the `psl` dependency is dropped (W14).
- **(3), (4) the surfaces:** the CLI, the owner's routes, the MCP and the inspector show and take a kind and a key.
- **(6) the fold:** an `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` entry writes no row; it admits its originator by the seed during the seed window, as (b) does today, logged by name.
- **(7) to (9):** the scenes, the charter statement and the build plan follow. L5 splits into L5a (the store and the four surfaces, no admission change) and L5b (the switch).

The keeper's two rows stand beside the design and come after L5: [#533](https://github.com/Calhooon/bsv-wallet-cli/issues/533) (expiry and spending ceilings per origin surfaced alike by every surface) and [#534](https://github.com/Calhooon/bsv-wallet-cli/issues/534) (a prompt surviving a brief reconnect within its own timeout).

**The #534 revision, of the same day.** [#534](https://github.com/Calhooon/bsv-wallet-cli/issues/534) is designed here, after L5b and L6 landed. The served wire carries no nonce of a request that a retry could repeat, so the prompt's key is the row's own identity, (originator, kind, key), and the revision adds only a window: a disconnect marks the originator's open rows abandoned for `ABANDON_WINDOW` (10 s), a call of the same originator and power inside it resumes the same row, and past it W4 (a)'s deny runs unchanged (question 2's "The brief reconnect's window"). What moved: (1) gains the column `abandoned_at`; (2) the window's paragraph, the cap's count of an abandoned row, the owner's answer refused while a row is abandoned; (7) scene 34 and scene 27's step 6; (8) the `disconnect` input with a time and the `expire` input; (9) the lane L8; the witness row W19.

## The reference, as read

**Four permission kinds, four admin baskets.** A grant is an on-chain PushDrop token in one of four baskets: `admin protocol-permission` (DPACP: protocol, security level, counterparty, privileged), `admin basket-access` (DBAP: basket), `admin certificate-access` (DCAP: verifier, type, fields, privileged), `admin spending-authorization` (DSAP: `authorizedAmount`, the month's ceiling) ([WPM:418-423](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/edf6e03dad39bd7bc4fc6e73e64de3a2b6f805c9/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/WalletPermissionsManager.ts#L418-L423), the token's fields [WPM:356-411](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/edf6e03dad39bd7bc4fc6e73e64de3a2b6f805c9/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/WalletPermissionsManager.ts#L356-L411)). Labels ride the protocol kind as `[1, "action label <label>"]` with the counterparty `self` and `privileged` false ([WPM:1817-1837](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/edf6e03dad39bd7bc4fc6e73e64de3a2b6f805c9/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/WalletPermissionsManager.ts#L1817-L1837)). There is no origin-level "connect" grant: a door the manager does not gate answers any originator.

**A power's key.** The manager names a request by its kind and instance ([WPM:5816-5828](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/edf6e03dad39bd7bc4fc6e73e64de3a2b6f805c9/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/WalletPermissionsManager.ts#L5816-L5828)): `proto:<originator>:<privileged>:<level>,<name>:<counterparty>`, `basket:<originator>:<basket>`, `cert:<originator>:<privileged>:<verifier>:<type>:<fields joined by |>`, `spend:<originator>:<satoshis>`. The rules that shape the instance:

## (1) The store

**The reference's answer.** Four kinds of on-chain token in four admin baskets, each one power: a protocol at a level with its counterparty and `privileged`, a basket, a certificate type from a verifier with its fields and `privileged`, and spending with its monthly ceiling. Each is keyed by the normalized hostname, with the raw name kept; an expiry in seconds, `0` never; spending's ceiling per calendar month, its tally read from labelled actions (above).

**The port's choice `[D]`, under W1 (a).** Three CLI-owned tables in the wallet's SQLite beside the toolbox's, in the module `origin_grants` (`src/store/origin_grants.rs`, the only writer of these tables). Every row of `origin_grants` is **one power**: an originator, a kind and the kind's instance key. The kinds are the reference's four; the scope `wallet` is withdrawn, and with it the `source` column (the fold writes no row, question 6, so every row is the owner's). The toolbox's tables are never touched: no `ALTER`, no index, no trigger on them, no foreign key into them.

```sql
CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS origin_grants (
  grant_id       INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
  originator     TEXT NOT NULL,          -- the key: the hostname, lowercased, no port, no scheme (WPM normalizeOriginator)
  raw_originator TEXT NOT NULL,          -- as named: the pending row's originator with its port, or the owner's argument
  kind           TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('protocol', 'basket', 'certificate', 'spending')),
  key            TEXT NOT NULL,          -- the instance, in the canonical text below; '[]' for spending
  amount         INTEGER,                -- spending only: the month's ceiling in satoshis
  granted_at     INTEGER NOT NULL,       -- unix seconds
  expiry         INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, -- unix seconds; 0 never expires
  revoked_at     INTEGER,                -- unix seconds; NULL is live
  CHECK ((kind = 'spending') = (amount IS NOT NULL)),
  CHECK (kind <> 'spending' OR (key = '[]' AND expiry = 0))
);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS origin_grants_power ON origin_grants (originator, kind, key);

CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS origin_pending (
  pending_id     INTEGER PRIMARY KEY,
  originator     TEXT NOT NULL,          -- the key, as above
  raw_originator TEXT NOT NULL,          -- the Origin's originator with its port (the reference's displayOriginator)
  kind           TEXT NOT NULL CHECK (kind IN ('protocol', 'basket', 'certificate', 'spending')),
  key            TEXT NOT NULL,          -- the power asked, as the grant row would hold it
  usage          TEXT,                   -- the usage of the call that wrote the row (signing, insertion, listing, ...); NULL for spending
  call           TEXT NOT NULL,          -- the door's path (/listOutputs)
  reason         TEXT,                   -- the call's reason as the reference passes it (description, privilegedReason)
  amount         INTEGER,                -- spending only: the call's satoshis
  renewal        INTEGER NOT NULL DEFAULT 0, -- 1: an expired grant, or a ceiling the call would pass
  first_seen     INTEGER NOT NULL,       -- unix seconds
  answered_at    INTEGER,                -- unix seconds; NULL is open
  answer         TEXT CHECK (answer IN ('grant', 'once', 'deny')),
  abandoned_at   INTEGER,                -- #534: unix seconds of the caller's disconnect; NULL while a caller is held or the row is answered
  CHECK ((kind = 'spending') = (amount IS NOT NULL)),
  CHECK (abandoned_at IS NULL OR (answered_at IS NULL AND kind <> 'spending'))
);
CREATE UNIQUE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS origin_pending_open ON origin_pending (originator, kind, key)
  WHERE answered_at IS NULL AND kind <> 'spending';

-- origin_spends and origin_spends_month: unchanged from L1 (the tally is per originator, as the spending row is).
```

JSON, rather than the reference's colon-joined form, because a protocol name holds spaces, a certificate type holds `/` and `=`, and a field name may hold any character: an array of escaped strings is the one text both unambiguous and readable. The module builds the key from a typed `Power` (the kind and its fields, proposed in `src/store/origin_grants/rows.rs`); a key text from the owner (question 4) is parsed and re-encoded, and refused naming its canonical form when it differs. `privileged` is kept although this wallet refuses every privileged request by kind before any key (`refuse_privileged`, `src/server/handlers.rs:136`), so every row the port writes carries `false`; the field stays so a key reads as the reference's and a privileged keyring later needs no migration.

**The lookup.** One indexed read of (originator, kind, key) for protocol, basket and spending. For a certificate, the originator's certificate rows with the same `privileged`, type and verifier (the unique index's (originator, kind) prefix) are read and the module keeps the rows whose field set holds every field the call asks, the reference's subset rule. The reading of the rows found, in order: a **live** row covering the power admits it; else a **revoked** row covering it refuses at once (W3); else an **expired** row covering it is a renewal; else **none**. `Standing` (`src/store/origin_grants/rows.rs`) keeps its four words; `lookup` (`src/store/origin_grants.rs:152`) takes a `Power` in place of a `Scope`.

**One row per power, one open prompt per power.** A grant is an upsert of its (originator, kind, key) row that sets `granted_at`, `expiry` and `amount` and clears `revoked_at`, as the stack's machine replaces a row on a new grant (`step`'s `grant` arm, `lean/BrowserGate.lean` lines 160-163 at `4372ea39`); a revoke stamps `revoked_at` and deletes nothing. A call asking a power with no live row joins the open pending row of the same (originator, kind, key) or writes one: the reference's queue join (`origin_pending_open`). Each spending call writes its own row, the reference's per-call spending key, so the index leaves spending out. The open-row key is the grant row's key, without the usage the reference appends: a call of another usage on the same power joins the open row (`[W]` W16). An answer stamps `answered_at` and `answer` and keeps the row, so the pending table is the history of the owner's answers. `once` answers a spending row alone, as the client offers a one-time grant for a spend alone. A row whose caller left is deleted, not answered (question 2): a spending row at once, any other row when its window passes with no call joining it (#534, question 2). The open-row index holds an abandoned row too (`answered_at IS NULL`), so a call of the same power inside the window joins it by the index already there.

**The migration of L1's tables `[D]`.** L1's `ensure` (`src/store/origin_grants.rs:95`) reads `sqlite_master` for its six objects (`OBJECTS`, `src/store/origin_grants.rs:36`) and creates the missing ones with `IF NOT EXISTS` (`DDL`, `src/store/origin_grants.rs:47`), so a store holding L1's `origin_grants` would keep its `scope` column and its `CHECK`, which SQLite cannot alter. The new `ensure` reads, on the reader, the six objects by type and name **and** whether `origin_grants` has the column `kind` (`has_column`, `src/store.rs:25`). That read is a fast path alone: case 1 below returns from it, and any other answer only sends `ensure` to the writer. **The case is decided on the writer, under its lock.** `ensure` runs lazily inside every row function (`lookup`, `grant`, `revoke`, `open_or_join`, `answer`, `listed`, `month_tally`), in every process that opens the store: the daemon, the CLI's `origin*` commands and `bsv-wallet ui`. Two of them can both read L1's shape on the reader; if each then decided from that read, the second's `ALTER TABLE origin_grants RENAME TO origin_grants_l1` would fail on a table the other had already moved. So after `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` (`write_tx`, which takes SQLite's write lock before any statement), `ensure` reads `sqlite_master` and `pragma_table_info('origin_grants')` again on the transaction's own connection (`has_column`'s query run on that connection, since `has_column` takes the reader's pool today) and decides its case from that second read. A writer that finds case 1 there (another process moved the store between its read and its lock) commits nothing and answers `false`. Four cases:

1. All six objects present and `kind` present: nothing is sent to the writer (as today).
2. No `origin_grants` table: the block above runs in one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction (`write_tx`, as today).
3. L1's shape (`origin_grants` present, `kind` absent): one `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transaction moves it. It renames `origin_grants` and `origin_pending` to `origin_grants_l1` and `origin_pending_l1` and drops L1's two indexes `origin_grants_key` and `origin_pending_open`. It runs the block above and copies each `spending` grant row of `origin_grants_l1` into the spending kind (key `[]`, its `amount`, `granted_at`, `revoked_at`, `expiry` 0). A `spending` row with a NULL `amount` (L1 allowed one for `source` `env` alone) is not copied, since the new `CHECK` refuses it. Then it drops the two `_l1` tables. `origin_spends` and its index stay as they are, since their shape does not move. The transaction is all or nothing: an interrupted move leaves L1's shape, and the next `ensure` moves it again.
4. Any other mix (the new `origin_grants` with a missing index or table): the block runs and gains what is missing, as L1's case does today.

**Where L1's tables stand today.** No release carries them: `git tag --contains` names no tag for any of PR 449's, 454's, 468's or 481's merge commits, so no tagged wallet holds these tables. `ensure` runs only lazily, inside each row function, never at start. The pending rows are written only by `open_or_join` (`src/store/origin_grants.rs:258`), whose callers at `bb9a742` are tests alone (`src/server/owner_door_tests.rs`, `src/commands/ui/tests.rs`, `tests/origins_cli.rs`). `fold` (`src/store/origin_grants.rs:449`) has no caller outside its tests. So a store has rows only where an owner ran an unreleased beta build's `origin-grant` (or the owner's routes, or the inspector) by hand. **The spending kind's amount.** Unchanged from L1's design (W6, W7): a call's amount is the sum of its requested outputs' satoshis, the measure the permission audit records (`satoshis` in `AuditEntry`, `src/server/audit.rs:35-52`). A call that needs the owner releases the lock while it waits, takes it again after the answer, and checks again. ## (2) The pending answer

**The reference's answer.** The HTTP call is held open with no timer until the owner acts (above). A grant resolves it into the door's own answer. A deny answers `400 {"message": "Permission denied."}` (the grouped denial's text is `The user has denied the request for permission.`). A disconnect writes nothing to the caller and denies every open prompt of its originator, every waiter on them included, keeping nothing. The bridge's death or the server's shutdown answers `503 WALLET_BRIDGE_UNAVAILABLE`.

**The port's choice `[D]`, under W4 (a) and rule 23.5 (c).** For each power a door asks (question 1), `powers::ask` reads the originator's rows:

- **live**: the power is admitted; the next power is asked, and the door runs when none is left, logged as today (`browser gate: admitted`, with each row's `grant_id`, kind and key in place of the env entry);
- **revoked**: refused at once, 400 `ERR_PERMISSION_DENIED`, `The user has denied the request for permission.` (`DENIED`), no pending row (the machine's `revoked`; `[W]` W3);
- **none, or expired**: the call joins the open pending row of the power or writes one (`renewal` 1 for an expired row), logged at info by name (`browser gate: pending`, `originator`, `kind`, `key`, `usage`, `call`, `pending_id`), and is **held**.

A held call waits, with no bound, for the earliest of four things:

- **The row answered.** `grant`: the call asks its next power. `deny`: 400 `ERR_PERMISSION_DENIED`, `Permission denied.`, the reference's per-request text. The client's protocol, basket and spending prompts deny through `denyPermission` ([ProtocolPermissionHandler:50](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/bsv-desktop/blob/b791cb2b571d62f23256e97abf97557739278a49/src/lib/components/ProtocolPermissionHandler/index.tsx#L50), [BasketAccessHandler:21](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/bsv-desktop/blob/b791cb2b571d62f23256e97abf97557739278a49/src/lib/components/BasketAccessHandler/index.tsx#L21)), whose error is `Permission denied.` ([WPM:1199](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/edf6e03dad39bd7bc4fc6e73e64de3a2b6f805c9/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/WalletPermissionsManager.ts#L1199)). The grouped text `DENIED` stays the revoked power's, a refusal no prompt answered.
- **The row gone.** The disconnect below deleted it: the same 400 `Permission denied.`, as `denyPermission` rejects every waiter.
- **The server's shutdown.** 503 `{"code": "WALLET_BRIDGE_UNAVAILABLE", "message": "WALLET_BRIDGE_UNAVAILABLE: HTTP server shutting down"}`, the client's code and text in the port's typed carrier (`[W]` W13). The call's open row is deleted, as the disconnect deletes it.
- **The caller's disconnect.** The connection's future is dropped: nothing is written, the door never runs, and the disconnect's deny follows (since #534, at the end of the row's window, below, unless a call joins the row inside it).

The wait reads its row every 250 ms (one indexed read; the CLI and the inspector are other processes) and wakes at once on the server's own answers (a `tokio::sync::Notify` per open row, kept in the server's `Held` map below, signalled by the owner's routes and by the disconnect). The shutdown is the server's `shutdown_signal` (`src/server/mod.rs:705`) passed to every waiter, because `with_graceful_shutdown` (`src/server/mod.rs:678`) waits for in-flight requests and a held call would otherwise hold the shutdown. A held call holds no lock: it waits before its handler's toolbox call and outside the `SpendingLock`.

**The disconnect, under W4 (a) `[D]`.** A held call carries a guard (`Abandon`, proposed) that does nothing when the call ends with any answer. The server keeps one map of the open rows its held calls wait on (`Held`, proposed, in the server's state behind a `std::sync::Mutex`): each `pending_id` with its originator's key and its `tokio::sync::Notify`, entered when a call opens or joins the row and removed when the row is answered or deleted. The open's commit and the map's entry run in one task the call's drop does not cut, which hands the armed guard to the call, so a caller gone during the commit drops the guard there and its row is withdrawn as any other (#552). When the call's future is dropped instead (the caller closed its connection), the guard's `Drop` takes, under that mutex and before it returns, a **snapshot** of the `pending_id`s the map holds for the originator's key, and spawns `origin_grants::abandon(originator, &ids, now)` (proposed) with that list. In one write transaction, `abandon` deletes those rows alone, of every kind, spending rows included, and only while they are still open (`DELETE FROM origin_pending WHERE originator = ? AND pending_id IN (...) AND answered_at IS NULL RETURNING pending_id`). It then signals each deleted row's `Notify`, so every other call held on one is answered 400 `Permission denied.`. It is logged at info by name (`browser gate: abandoned`, `originator`, the deleted `pending_id`s with their kind and key). No grant row is written and no pending row is kept: the reference's denial keeps nothing, so the port's history holds the owner's answers alone. The snapshot is the bound: the spawned task runs later than the drop, and a delete by originator alone would also take a row that a live call of the same page opened in between, denying a caller that never left, against "No later auto-deny" below. A live call that joins a snapshotted row before the task runs is answered `Permission denied.` with it, as a waiter on a prompt the client denies is.

- **Per originator, as the client.** The client denies per originator, not per request (httpBridgeSession.ts lines 1-8, PermissionQueueManager.ts lines 217-246, above), so one tab's disconnect denies a second tab's prompt of the same page, as the client's own header says it may.
- **On every port of the host.** The port applies that rule on its key, the hostname. The client's own match misses a page on an explicit port (the edge above), so the port denies in a case where the client denies nothing; the client's stated intent is per originator, and the port's originator is the hostname (W4).
- **No later auto-deny.** The client also denies a prompt raised later while every session of its originator is cancelled. The port has no such window: a call after the disconnect is a live caller, never denied for its page's earlier disconnect. Since #534, inside the abandoned row's window (below) a call of the same originator asking the same power rejoins that row and is held on the same `pending_id` (scene 34), and a call asking another power opens its own prompt; past the window the row is gone and the call opens a new prompt (scene 27 step 6).
- **Racing the owner.** A row the owner answered before the deletion is not deleted (`answered_at IS NULL`). An answer arriving after it finds no row: the owner is told `no pending request #N` (`Refused::NoSuchPending`), which the routes serve as 400 `VALIDATION_ERROR` today. A deleted row leaves no trace to tell it from an id never written, so L5b keeps the answer and widens its words: `no pending request #N (a request is withdrawn when its caller disconnects)`. The wire fixture `tests/wire/196-owner.answer.noSuchPending.json` holds today's words (`no pending request #1`), so it moves with that change, in its own commit (the fixtures below).

**The restart.** No caller survives a restart. At the start of `serve` and `daemon`, after the store opens and before the server binds, `origin_grants::sweep_open(now)` (proposed) deletes every open pending row and logs their count by name (`origin grants: N open requests from a previous run withdrawn; their callers are gone`). The reference's queue lives in the renderer's memory and ends with it. So an open row always has a caller waiting on it, from the start of a run until its answer or its caller's disconnect, and an abandoned row (below) lives no longer than its window. The sweep is unchanged by #534: it deletes open and abandoned rows alike (`answered_at IS NULL`).

**The brief reconnect's window (#534) `[D]`, `[W]` W19.** The keeper's [#534](https://github.com/Calhooon/bsv-wallet-cli/issues/534), of 2026-11-03, beside W4 (a): a page can lose its connection for a moment (a reload, a flaky network, a fetch the page aborts and sends again) and come back to the same request; the reference denies it and the page starts over. This paragraph lets the prompt survive a brief reconnect within a window the revision defines, and leaves W4 (a) exactly as it is past the window.

- **What the served wire carries.** The row asked for "the request's own" nonce: the BRC-103/104 nonce where the wire carries one, else the port's request id. Neither can match a retry. The MetaNet Client's bridge receives a plain HTTP request and hands the renderer its method, path, headers, body and a `request_id` it mints itself (`requestIdCounter++`, [httpServer.ts:27](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/bsv-desktop/blob/b791cb2b571d62f23256e97abf97557739278a49/electron/httpServer.ts#L27), [httpServer.ts:166-171](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/bsv-desktop/blob/b791cb2b571d62f23256e97abf97557739278a49/electron/httpServer.ts#L166-L171)). The page never sends that id or sees it, and a reconnect is a new HTTP request with a new id. The bridge's doors call the manager with the parsed origin alone (`wallet.isAuthenticated({}, origin)`, [onWalletReady.ts:762-770](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/bsv-desktop/blob/b791cb2b571d62f23256e97abf97557739278a49/src/onWalletReady.ts#L762-L770), and every door after it): no BRC-103 handshake, no BRC-104 `x-bsv-auth` header, no session. The port's served wire is the same, an `Origin` and a JSON body: `auth_middleware` (`src/server/mod.rs`) compares one static bearer, the owner's, and keeps no session, as the open doors' section (#541) found. A request id the port minted would, like the bridge's, name one HTTP request and never its retry. So the key is the request's identity as the gate already reads it: the originator from the `Origin`, which a browser page cannot set (a forbidden header), and the power the door asks.
- **The decision: the row's own identity (shape A).** A prompt is already one open row per (originator, kind, key) (question 1), and a call asking a power with an open row joins it, the reference's queue join ([WPM:2614-2625](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/edf6e03dad39bd7bc4fc6e73e64de3a2b6f805c9/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/WalletPermissionsManager.ts#L2614-L2625)). A reconnecting page's retry asks the same power from the same originator, so it reaches the same row by the index already there; the revision adds only the window, and no wire field, no second identity, no new key. **Shape B**, a nonce the page sends (a header or a body field repeated on its retry, keyed with the originator), would be a wire field the reference and the client do not carry, which no reference page sends, so it would serve no page written for the client; it adds a value the page chooses, so its forgery reading is the originator's alone (a page can only name its own nonces) and its replay reading needs a nonce store with its own bound and expiry. **Shape C**, a content key (the originator, the door and the canonical arguments hashed), adds no field but a second identity beside the row's: two calls asking one power with different arguments would be two prompts where the row's index makes one (against W16's join), and a retry whose arguments differ in a byte the power does not read (a `description`, an output's script) would miss its prompt. Shape A costs nothing on the wire and nothing in identity; its cost is W19's below.
- **A row's states.** **Open**: `answered_at` and `abandoned_at` NULL, a call held on it. **Abandoned**: `answered_at` NULL, `abandoned_at` the unix seconds of the disconnect, no call needed on it. **Answered**: `answered_at` and `answer` set, the history. A row is deleted from open or abandoned (the window's end, the shutdown, the restart's sweep) and never from answered.
- **The window.** `ABANDON_WINDOW` = **10 s** (proposed, `src/server/powers.rs` after the pin, beside `READ_EVERY`), a constant of the module, the row's to change, never a page's or a setting's. Why 10 s: a reload aborts the page's fetches, loads the page and sends its calls again, which takes a second or two on a warm cache and a few seconds on a cold load or a slow machine; a page retrying with a doubling backoff (1, 2 and 4 s) reaches its third retry at 7 s. A longer window buys little for those and costs the bound: an abandoned row holds a place under the cap and a line in the owner's list (below), and every call still held on it waits the window for the same denial the reference gives at once. A row is inside its window while `now < abandoned_at + ABANDON_WINDOW` and past it from then on.
- **What the disconnect does now.** `Waiting`'s drop (the design's `Abandon`) takes the snapshot of the originator's rows under `Held`'s mutex, as today, and its task calls `origin_grants::abandon(originator, &ids, now)`, which in one write transaction (a) deletes the snapshot's open spending rows at once, as today: a spending row is one call's (the reference's per-call key, [WPM:5830-5840](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/edf6e03dad39bd7bc4fc6e73e64de3a2b6f805c9/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/WalletPermissionsManager.ts#L5830-L5840)), so no retry can join it; and (b) marks the snapshot's other open rows `abandoned_at = now` (`UPDATE ... WHERE originator = ? AND pending_id IN (...) AND answered_at IS NULL AND abandoned_at IS NULL`: a row already abandoned keeps its earlier time, so a second disconnect never lengthens a window). Each deleted row's `Notify` is signalled (its calls answered 400 `Permission denied.`); each marked row's calls keep waiting. Logged at info by name: `browser gate: abandoned` for the deleted rows, as today, and `browser gate: disconnected` for the marked ones (`originator`, the `pending_id`s with kind and key, `until` the window's end). The task then sleeps `ABANDON_WINDOW` and calls `origin_grants::expire(originator, &marked, abandoned_at)` (proposed).
- **What the window's end does.** `expire` deletes, in one write transaction, the marked rows still abandoned **with the same mark** (`... AND answered_at IS NULL AND abandoned_at = ?`), so a row a call rejoined (its mark cleared) or a row marked again later (a later time) is left to its own timer. It signals each deleted row's `Notify`: every call still held on one is answered 400 `Permission denied.`, as W4 (a)'s deny answers it today, and logs `browser gate: abandoned`, its words unchanged. No grant row is written and no pending row is kept. Past the window the result is W4 (a)'s exactly, the same rows deleted and the same calls denied, 10 s later.
- **Nothing outlives the window, without the timer.** The task can fail (the store busy, the process killed between the mark and the timer). So every reader treats a row past its window as gone: `open_or_join` deletes an abandoned row of the power past its window in its own transaction before it writes a new row (the next call removes it); `listing`, `owners_listing` and `pending` read `abandoned_at IS NULL OR abandoned_at > now - ABANDON_WINDOW`, so no surface shows such a row; `answer` finds no such row and says `no pending request #N (a request is withdrawn when its caller disconnects)`, its words unchanged (fixture 196 does not move). The restart's sweep deletes whatever is left.
- **A call inside the window.** A call of the same originator (any port of the host, the key) asking the same power finds the abandoned row through `open_or_join`'s read of the open row, clears `abandoned_at` in the same transaction and is held on the same `pending_id`, logged `browser gate: pending` with `joined = true` and `resumed = true`. The owner sees the same prompt with the same id and the same `first_seen`, and the owner's later answer answers the rejoined call once, as any joined call. A call of another originator asking the same power writes its own row: the originator is part of the index, so no page reaches another origin's row, whatever it sends. A spending call inside the window writes its own row, as every spending call does.
- **The owner's answer inside the window: refused by name, the captain's word of 2026-11-04.** Refused while the row is abandoned: `answer` reads `abandoned_at` in its transaction and refuses `pending request #N is waiting for its page to reconnect; answer it when the page asks again, or it is withdrawn at <abandoned_at + ABANDON_WINDOW>`, a new word (`Refused::Abandoned`, proposed), served 409 `CONFLICT` as `AlreadyAnswered` is, by the owner's routes and the CLI's `origin-grant` and `origin-deny` alike; its `UPDATE` gains `AND abandoned_at IS NULL`, so the answer and the mark race to one winner (a row answered before the mark is never marked, since the mark reads `answered_at IS NULL`). The design brief's sketch let the answer stand for the rejoining call and be withdrawn with the row at the window's end; but a `grant` writes the power's grant row in the answer's own transaction (`answer`, `src/store/origin_grants.rs` after the pin), so standing would either grant a page that has left, the cost W4 (a) refused, or need a deferred grant held on the pending row with its amount and expiry and withdrawn at the window's end, a second write path for one answer. Refusing keeps one path and W4 (a) whole: no answer of the owner ever reaches a page that has left, and no grant row is written for a page that may never return, which W4 (a)'s "no row is kept" forbids. Its cost is a second answer when the owner acts in the seconds a page reloads. The captain's word of 2026-11-04 settles the lane's question on this design: the answer is refused by name, never held to stand for a rejoining call, and the 10 s of `ABANDON_WINDOW` stands as reasoned above, measured by L8 on a real reload and on the six-connection shuffle (question 9). The surfaces show the state: a pending row's JSON carries `abandonedAt` only while the row is abandoned (absent on an open row, so no recorded fixture moves), the CLI's line and the inspector's row end `caller disconnected, withdrawn at <time> unless the page asks again`, and the inspector disables the row's answer buttons.
- **The cap's count.** An abandoned row holds the owner's list as an open one does, so it holds a place under W14's cap: a held call's place (`Place`) passes, when the last call held on a row leaves by disconnect, to the abandoned row, which keeps it until its window ends or a call joins it; the joining call takes that place over and asks no new one. A row with a call still held on it needs no place of its own (the call's covers it). So every open or abandoned row holds at least one place, a page's places (its held calls and its abandoned rows with no call held on them) are at most 2, the wallet's at most 4, and the owner's list holds at most 4 such rows at any time, as before #534. A page that disconnects and reconnects inside every window keeps its row as a page holding a call keeps it, within the same 2 places, and never more. The 429 past the cap is unchanged.
- **The shutdown.** Unchanged: every held call is answered 503 and its row deleted; an abandoned row with no call is left to the restart's sweep, and no surface shows it past its window meanwhile.

The revision changes no recorded fixture: a disconnect writes nothing to its caller, the rejoined call's answer is a held call's answer (the fixtures of question 2), `abandonedAt` is absent on every recorded pending row, and fixture 196's words stand. The new fixture is the owner's answer refused while a row is abandoned (409 `CONFLICT`, the word above), recorded with an owner step that marks a row as the disconnect does, in its own commit.

**The cap on held calls `[D]`, `[W]` W14, revisited.** L1's design capped pending rows per registrable domain (at most 4 open and 4 new a minute, 64 in all) because rows outlived their callers and a page minting subdomains could flood the owner's durable list. Under W4 (a) that cost is gone: an open row exists only while a call is held on it (or, since #534, for its window, holding its call's place), so the rows are bounded by the held calls' places. What remains is the browser's connections. A browser opens at most six HTTP/1.1 connections to the wallet's host and port, which every page in that browser shares, so a page holding six calls starves the granted pages. The reference has the same exposure and no cap. The cap shrinks to the held calls alone:

- an originator (the key, the hostname) holds at most 2 places at once, and the wallet at most 4 in all; a place is a held call (a spending call held inside `createAction` counts as one; a joined call counts, since it holds a connection) or, since #534, an abandoned row with no call held on it (the place its last call passed to it, above);
- so at most 4 places hold calls, at least 2 of the browser's six connections stay free for granted pages, whatever a hostile page does; and the owner's list holds at most 4 open or abandoned rows at any time.

A call past either limit is answered at once and never held: 429 `{"code": "ERR_PERMISSION_PENDING_LIMIT", "message": "Too many permission requests from this site are waiting for the owner's answer."}` with `Retry-After: 5` and the CORS echo, so the page can read it. No row is written, and the refusal is logged at warn by name (`browser gate: pending limit`, `originator`, `call`, the limit reached). The owner's answer to a row, or its caller's disconnect, frees its places at once. A revoked power is refused before the count, and a live one is never counted. The registrable domain and with it **the `psl` dependency are dropped**: without durable rows there is no flood across subdomains to count, and the wallet-wide limit alone keeps the two connections free. The cost is fairness between sites, not safety. A page using two subdomains can take the wallet's 4 places, and other pages' new prompts are refused 429 until the owner answers or that page leaves; granted pages are never held, so they keep their connections.

## The open doors' bound (#541)

The owner ruled W15 (i) on 2026-11-03 ("okay go with (i) for W15 if its god tier and ndorth star"): the open doors and level 0 answer any page with a parsed `Origin` and an agreeing originator, with no row. This section bounds them per originator, so an admitted page cannot hammer them; the reference has no such bound, and the port's cost is a page past it (below). `[D]`, built in `src/server/open_bounds.rs`.

**The order.** The gate's order is unchanged: the `Origin` and the agreement, then the seed (a seed-listed originator is admitted at every door before any bound, as (b) admits it: the outer allow-list stays the gate), then the bound, at the seam L5b named (`powers::open`, `// #541`), then the door. The owner's routes, the host routes' owner (no `Origin`, a loopback `Host`) and so the MCP's host calls never reach the seam and are never bounded.

**The figures, and why.** Each bound is keyed by the originator's hostname, the grants' key (`key_of`, every port of a host one originator), so a page cannot multiply its allowance by port.

| class | doors | figure |
|---|---|---|
| presence | `isAuthenticated`, `waitForAuthentication` (the port reads no manifest, W11) | one answer per door per originator a minute |
| chain | `getHeight`, `getHeaderForHeight`, `getNetwork`, `getVersion`, one bound for the four | 60 a minute per originator, a burst of 10 |
| level 0 | a protocol power at security level 0, at any door, one bound | 60 a minute per originator, a burst of 10 |

- **The presence doors' session is a minute.** The proposal was the authenticated transport's session where one exists. The served auth layer keeps none: `auth_middleware` (`src/server/mod.rs`) compares one static bearer, the owner's, which never names a page and never admits an origin (#411 rule 4), and the port serves no BRC-31 or BRC-103 handshake that would give a page a session id. So a presence door answers each originator once a minute, which is a page's opening `isAuthenticated` and `waitForAuthentication` and nothing after. The two doors are counted apart so the opening pair both answer.
- **The chain doors at 60 a minute with a burst of 10.** A page reads the network and the version when it opens and the height or a header as it works; one a second sustained covers a page that follows the chain, and ten at once cover its opening reads. The four doors share one bound: they are one class of public chain facts, and a page cannot spread its calls over them to read four times as often.
- **The level-0 powers the same, on a bound of their own.** A level-0 signature or key costs the wallet a derivation, as a chain read costs it a header service call. Its own bound keeps a page's level-0 use from spending its chain reads, and the reverse.
- **The counters live in this process's memory**, never in the store: a restart resets them, and a bounded call writes no row of any table: the bound is checked before the probe record counts the answer (#539, the section below), so a refused call is neither answered nor counted. The figures are constants of the module, the row's to change, never a page's or a setting's. Past 1024 originators' counters, the idle ones (whose window has passed, the same as none) are dropped before a new one is kept, so a page minting hostnames cannot grow the map without bound.

**The rule.** Each bound is the generic cell rate algorithm over an emission interval `T` (60 s for presence, 1 s for chain and level 0) and a burst `b` (1, 10, 10): a call is admitted while its theoretical arrival time is at most `(b - 1) * T` ahead of now, and each admitted call moves that time on by `T`; a refused call moves nothing. So the tenth chain call at once is admitted and the eleventh refused for 1 s, and a presence door's second call within the minute is refused for the minute's rest.

**What is charged (#556).** An open door is charged only by a method its route answers (`open_bounds::OPEN_DOORS` carries each door's methods, the gate's map, pinned to the router by a test): a call by another method is the router's `405`, neither counted nor charged. A door's level-0 powers read the bound each in its place (past it, the 429 at once), and the call is charged once, and its powers counted, only when every power it asks has admitted it, so a later refusal leaves the counter where it stood.

**The answer past a bound.** At once, never held: `429 {"code": "ERR_RATE_LIMITED", "message": "Too many calls to /getHeight from this site: the chain doors (getHeight, getHeaderForHeight, getNetwork, getVersion) answer 60 a minute per site, 10 at once; retry after 1 s."}`, the message naming the door and the window (a level-0 call's names `level-0 calls to <door>` and its own window, a presence door's `a presence door answers once a minute per site`), with `Retry-After` the wait in whole seconds rounded up (at least 1) and the CORS echo; the port's CORS layer already exposes `retry-after` to pages. It is logged at warn by name (`browser gate: rate limited`, `originator`, `door`, `class`, `retry_after`). The carrier is the port's one carrier, as W5 and W13.

**Cost.** A page reloaded within the minute reads 429 at the presence doors until its `Retry-After`; a page polling a chain door faster than once a second reads 429 for the rest of that second. A page using two hostnames has two allowances, as W14's cap. The reference answers all of these without a bound.

## (8) The charter transition's statement, for the keeper

"A door admits a page for each power it asks by the owner's grant of that power to its origin." Over the rows, with `host(o)` the originator's hostname (the key), `now` the call's time in seconds, `r.powers` the powers the door asks of the call (question 1's map, read by the host from the door and its arguments) and `seed` the seed window's list `[D]`:

```lean
-- A power: a kind and its instance key, in the canonical text.
structure Power where
  kind : String          -- "protocol" | "basket" | "certificate" | "spending"
  key  : String

-- A row covers a power: the same kind and key, or for a certificate the same
-- privileged, type and verifier with every asked field among the row's.
def covers (g : Grant) (p : Power) : Bool := g.kind == p.kind && keyCovers g.kind g.key p.key

-- A power is live for an origin at a time.
def live (gs : List Grant) (o : Origin) (p : Power) (now : Time) : Bool :=
  gs.any (fun g => g.origin == host o && covers g p && g.revokedAt.isNone && !expired g now)

-- The door's predicate (every door but the exempt callback and the owner's routes).
def admits (token : Bool) (seed : List Origin) (r : Request) (gs : List Grant) : Prop :=
  (token = true → r.bearer = true) ∧
  -- a host route: the owner's admission, or a seed-listed origin in the window
  (r.hostRoute = true → r.owner = true ∨ ∃ o, r.origin = some o ∧ agrees r.claimed o = true ∧ host o ∈ seed) ∧
  (r.hostRoute = false →
  ∃ o, r.origin = some o ∧ agrees r.claimed o = true ∧
    (host o ∈ seed ∨
     ∀ p ∈ r.powers, (p.kind = "spending" → r.once = true ∨ ∃ g ∈ gs, g.origin = host o ∧
          covers g p = true ∧ g.revokedAt = none ∧ ∃ a, g.amount = some a ∧ r.spent + r.amount ≤ a) ∧
        (p.kind ≠ "spending" → live gs o p r.now = true)))

-- The statement: an if-and-only-if. A call reaches its door exactly when `admits` holds.
theorem reaches_iff (token : Bool) (seed : List Origin) (r : Request) (gs : List Grant) :
    (door token seed r gs).admits = true ↔ admits token seed r gs
```

The fields the host supplies, none of them sent by the page:

- `r.hostRoute`: the path is one of the host routes (`/permission-audit`, `/permission-audit/reset`, `/chain-feed`, `/tx/{txid}`). Their `r.powers` is `[]`, since no kind names them, so without this conjunct `admits` would hold for any page with an agreeing origin, against question 1's map (400 `DENIED`). A host route admits the owner (`r.owner`) or, in the window, a seed-listed origin; never a page by its rows.
- `r.owner`: the owner's admission as `owner_door::refusal` reads it (no `Origin`, a loopback `Host`, the bearer under `AUTH_TOKEN`).
- `r.once`: the owner answered `once` the spending pending row this call holds; the host reads it from that row's `answer`, never from the request.
- `r.spent`: the originator's tally for the UTC month of `r.now` (`origin_spends`, `month_tally`), read by the host inside the `SpendingLock`; `r.amount` is the call's amount under W6, the outputs' sum.

It is an if-and-only-if, not an implication. The `→` direction says only the grant of every power the door asks reaches it (a seed-listed origin aside, in the window); it is `admitted_only_by_live_grant` (line 238) lifted to powers, with the bearer lock ahead. The `←` direction says the powers' live rows, the bearer when one is set, and an agreeing claim are enough; it is `granted_admitted` (line 192) per power. A door that asks no power (`r.powers = []`: the open doors and level 0, the reference's reading of W15; and a `createAction` whose outputs sum to 0, W6's divergence, where the reference asks spending for the fee) admits every page with a parsed origin and an agreeing claim, the host routes aside (below); under the strict reading the statement gains a conjunct for those doors. The bearer clause is the lock in front of the gate (`door`, line 125): it can only refuse, so it appears as a conjunct and never as an alternative to a grant (`bearer_changes_no_word`, line 295).

What it asks of `lean/BrowserGate.lean` at `4372ea39`:

- `host : Origin → Origin`, the originator's hostname. At the pin `Origin` is already the hostname with its port dropped (line 45), so `host` is the identity there. It is named so the statement survives an `Origin` that keeps its port.
- `Grant` (line 53): `scope : String` gives way to `kind` and `key`, and `amount : Option Nat` is added; `Power` and `covers` are added, with `keyCovers` the certificate's subset rule and equality otherwise.
- `Request` (line 65) gains `powers : List Power`; `amount`, `spent` and `once` for the spending power (the call's W6 amount, the month's tally and the owner's answer, all supplied by the host); and `hostRoute` and `owner` for the host routes.
- `rowFor` (line 87) is keyed by (origin, power) where it reads the origin alone, and `gate` (line 102) judges each power of `r.powers` in order; the earliest power not live gives the word (`pending`, `expired` or `revoked`).
- `door` (line 125) gains the `seed` argument.

A `grant` (the owner's answer) on an abandoned pair is refused and changes nothing (question 2). With them `call_writes_no_grant` (line 264) still holds, since only `grant` writes a grant, and W4 (a)'s theorem holds past the window:

```lean
-- `run is s` steps the inputs `is` in order; `callOf i o p` holds when the
-- input i is a call of o asking p (names proposed for the keeper).
-- After o disconnects at t, with no call asking p after it from any origin of
-- o's host (a call from another port of the host rejoins the pair), the state
-- at any t' from t + W on holds no pending pair (host o, p), open or abandoned.
theorem disconnect_denies_past_window (s : State) (o : Origin) (p : Power)
    (t t' : Time) (is : List Input) (hw : t + W ≤ t')
    (quiet : ∀ i ∈ is, ∀ o' : Origin, host o' = host o → ¬ callOf i o' p) :
    ∀ q ∈ (step (expire t') (run is (step (disconnect o t) s))).pending,
      ¬ (q.origin = host o ∧ q.power = p)

-- The disconnect and the window's end write no grant.
theorem disconnect_writes_no_grant (s : State) (o : Origin) (t : Time) :
    (step (disconnect o t) s).grants = s.grants
theorem expire_writes_no_grant (s : State) (t : Time) :
    (step (expire t) s).grants = s.grants
```

The keeper's Lean form needs the window: at `4372ea39` the state's `pending` is a `List Origin` with no time (line 132) and the machine reads no clock but the expiry's, so the pair's `abandonedAt`, the `expire` input and `W` are new; the statement is the one the port builds to, not a change to the stack. A pending pair joined inside the window is the same pair, so the scenes' rows read one `pending_id` across the reconnect.

Every existing theorem holds per power with the powers list of one element; the scenarios' rows move from the scope `wallet` to the kinds (question 7). The machine's `pending` and `expired` words are the host's held call on the wire (question 2), and its `revoked` is the immediate refusal. `call_writes_no_grant` holds of the port because only the module's grant and answer functions write `origin_grants`, and neither is reachable from a page (question 3). The seed is configuration, not a row: it is the statement's `seed` argument, empty when the variable is unset and after L7. Rule 8 is the keeper's: this is the statement the port builds to, not a change to the stack.

## The probe record (#539)

One of the three refinements of the owner's W15 (i) ruling of 2026-11-03 ("okay go with (i) for W15 if its god tier and ndorth star"), shipping before the mainnet gate of the release carrying L5b. Every answer the gate gives with no row is counted per origin as a row of record, never a grant, so the owner sees which admitted pages probe and when: the open doors (`isAuthenticated`, `waitForAuthentication` without a manifest, `getHeight`, `getHeaderForHeight`, `getNetwork`, `getVersion`) and every protocol power at security level 0. A seed-listed originator's answers are the seed's and are not counted; a call that asks a power of a row is not a probe.

- **The table.** `origin_probes`, the module's fourth table, made by `ensure` as the other three are (`IF NOT EXISTS`, in `OBJECTS` and `DDL`; a store of L5b's shape gains it and keeps its rows; the toolbox's tables untouched): one row per (originator, door, power) under the unique index `origin_probes_seen`, with `raw_originator` (the name last given), `count`, `first_seen` and `last_seen` (unix seconds). The originator is the hostname key of every other row; the door is its name without the slash (`getVersion`, `createSignature`); the power is a level-0 protocol's key in the protocol's canonical text with the level 0 and no counterparty (`[false,0,"browser gate",""]`, `Power::level_zero_key`, never a grant's key), and empty at an open door (`null` in JSON).
- **The cap.** `probes::PER_ORIGINATOR` = **32** rows per originator, its remainder row among them. Past it, the rows seen longest ago (by `last_seen`, then the older row) fold into the originator's one remainder row (door `*`, no power): their counts summed, `first_seen` the earliest and `last_seen` the latest. The reasoning: a page's honest probes are the six open doors and the few level-0 protocols it uses, so 32 holds every one with room to spare and the remainder is reached only by a page minting protocol names; 32 lines is one screen of `bsv-wallet origins` per page; and each write folds in the same transaction, so a page calling a door in a loop writes one row and a page minting names never holds more than 32. The table is bounded by the cap times the originators the browser sends, each a page the owner's browser opened.
- **The off-path write.** The gate never waits on the record. At the seam L5b named (`powers::open`, reached from the gate's open doors and from `Asker::ask`'s level-0 arm), the answer is a `try_send` on a bounded queue (`server::probes::Probes`, 1024 answers) and the call goes on at once; one task per served router drains the queue and writes up to 64 answers in one transaction (`probes::record`). A failed write is logged at warn (`browser gate: the probe record was not written`) and lost, never an error to the page; a full queue drops the answer's count, and the next write logs how many were dropped. The task ends with its router. The gate never reads the table: no admission depends on it, and `origin-revoke` and the owner's revoke leave it (they stamp grant rows).
- **The surfaces.** A `probes` section of its own, distinct from the grants and the pending rows, in the listings alone, read through the module (`probes::probes`, by originator, its remainder last, then door and power): `bsv-wallet origins` prints one line a row (`probe  new.example  getVersion  2 times  first 2026-11-03T18:00Z  last 2026-11-03T18:01Z`; the level-0 power after the door; the remainder `* (older rows folded)`), and `--json` carries `probes: [{originator, rawOriginator, door, power, count, firstSeen, lastSeen}]`, times in unix seconds; `GET /origins` and the MCP's `list_origins` (the same body), and the inspector's `/origins` view (a probes table under the grants, from `/api/origins`). The answers of `origin-grant`, `origin-deny`, `origin-revoke` and the owner's three `POST` routes carry no `probes` key.
- **The proofs.** The store's tests (`src/store/origin_grants/probes/tests.rs`: one row per door and power with its count and times, a thousand polls one row; the cap's fold and a row seen again kept; the table made by `ensure` and left by a revoke); the router's (`src/server/probes_tests.rs`: a page's open doors and level 0 counted and never a grant, the seed's never counted; a call answered at once while the store's writer is held, and a write the store refuses lost to the log, the page answered); the CLI's `--json` and line (`tests/origins_cli.rs`), the inspector's (`src/commands/ui/tests.rs`); the wire fixtures 192 and 194 (`probes: []`) and 210 to 213 (two `getVersion` calls and a level-0 `createSignature` from a page holding no row, then `GET /origins` with two probe rows, counts 2 and 1); scene 32 on wallet D.
