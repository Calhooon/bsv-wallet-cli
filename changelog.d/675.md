- **A broadcaster's refusal is a hint; a retire needs the definitive absence; a fault is not an answer (For #660).** Under `serve`, a `stale` row's stored BEEF is announced once; when a broadcaster refuses that announce, the refusal is now kept on the row as a hint (`<provider>|rejected`, or `<provider>|double_spend` with the competitors it names). The word stays `stale` with its re-ask. Before, the refusal became the row's stored verdict (`rejected` or `conflicted`), and `tracker-tick` and the served pass's log line counted it under `verdict(s)`; they now count it under `refused (a hint)`, and `tracker-tick --json` carries the count under the key `stale_refused` where it carried `verdicts`. A verdict an earlier release stored on a row is read as stored.
- **Fewer retires on a refused transaction (For #660).** The served sweep (the pass of `serve` and `daemon`, and the sweep after `cleanup-abandoned`) no longer retires an `unproven` or `sending` transaction because one broadcaster's `rejected` row names it. `reconcile-broadcasts --execute` no longer retires one on that row before probing. A transaction is retired only on the definitive absence, which is the broadcaster's own negative and every voting chain index's clean 404. The follow-up after a served broadcast reads it in both of its forms (the broadcaster's REJECTED word, or its answer that it holds no record of the transaction); `reconcile-broadcasts` retires on the REJECTED form and keeps a transaction the broadcaster holds no record of, which `cleanup-abandoned` retires. A broadcaster's REJECTED beside a chain index that could not be read (a 5xx, a 429, a timeout) or that is not configured no longer retires anything either. `reconcile-broadcasts` names such a transaction under `refusalHints` in its JSON and `refusal_hints=` in its log line, and keeps it. A refused transaction is therefore retired later, or not at all, where one refusal retired it before. Meanwhile the coins it spent stay locked. They stay locked until a proof, a competitor's checked proof, or the definitive absence decides the transaction. `reconcile-broadcasts --execute` (the REJECTED form), `cleanup-abandoned` and the daemon's abandoned-transaction ticker read that absence. An operator of a store run under `serve` alone, which has no such ticker: a transaction an earlier release would have retired on its `rejected` row now waits; run `cleanup-abandoned` (with `--execute` once its dry run reads right) after the upgrade and whenever a refused transaction's coins should come back. A retired transaction keeps its raw bytes, as before. This is an interim: the cut replaces release with replacement.
